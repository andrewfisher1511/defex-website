WATERPROOFING·3 JUL 2026·ANDREW FISHER

Why balcony leaks come back

When a balcony leaks a second time, the usual explanation is that the repair "failed". In most of the buildings I inspect, that is not what happened — the repair fixed something that was never the entry point. Water tracks along slabs, through hobs and behind finishes, and shows itself a long way from where it got in.

The most common true causes are terminations and falls, not the membrane field: an edge detail that stops short, a door threshold with no upturn, falls that pond water against the very junction least able to hold it. Patching the wet patch treats the symptom and leaves the cause collecting water for next summer.

The fix is diagnostic, not heroic: targeted opening-up to confirm the path, then a design that re-establishes falls and details every termination. It costs more than a patch and considerably less than a third repair.

DBP ACT·19 JUN 2026·ANDREW FISHER

Declared designs, in plain terms

If your building is Class 2 — broadly, apartments — the Design and Building Practitioners Act 2020 changed what has to exist before remedial work touches a building element like waterproofing or structure: a regulated design, prepared and declared by a Registered Design Practitioner, lodged before work starts.

In practice this means the era of a contractor "working it out on site" is over for this class of work. The design has to be resolved on paper first — which is precisely where remedial work goes right or wrong anyway. The Act, whatever else it did, made good practice mandatory.

For owners corporations the practical question is sequencing: engage the design before the builder, not after. A declared design lets you tender like for like, and it is the document your building keeps for the decades the repair is meant to last.

CONTRACTS·5 JUN 2026·ANDREW FISHER

The cheapest tender is rarely the cheapest job

When three tenders come back wildly apart, the instinct is to celebrate the low one. The better question is what each builder thinks they are pricing. A one-page scope gives every tenderer a different job to imagine — and the low bid usually belongs to the most optimistic imagination.

The gap gets settled later, as variations, priced without competitive tension after the contract is signed. That is where a cheap tender becomes an expensive job.

A measured specification inverts this: everyone prices the same defined work, prices land close together, and the variations that remain are genuine unknowns rather than gaps. On remedial projects the specification usually pays for itself before the scaffold is up.
