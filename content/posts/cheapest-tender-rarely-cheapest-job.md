When three tenders come back wildly apart, the instinct is to celebrate the low one. The better question is what each builder thinks they are pricing. A one-page scope gives every tenderer a different job to imagine — and the low bid usually belongs to the most optimistic imagination.

The gap gets settled later, as variations, priced without competitive tension after the contract is signed. That is where a cheap tender becomes an expensive job.

A measured specification inverts this: everyone prices the same defined work, prices land close together, and the variations that remain are genuine unknowns rather than gaps. On remedial projects the specification usually pays for itself before the scaffold is up.
